- ## Filaments
	- ### Comparison
		- | Filament | Nozzle °C | Bed °C | Softens | Enclosure |
		  |---|---|---|---|---|
		  | PLA | 190–220 | 50–60 | ~55–60 °C | No |
		  | PETG | 220–250 | 70–85 | ~75–80 °C | No |
		  | ABS | 230–260 | 90–110 | ~100 °C | Yes |
		  | ASA | 240–260 | 90–110 | ~100 °C | Yes |
		  | TPU | 210–230 | 30–60 | n/a | No |
		  | Nylon | 250–280 | 70–100 | ~80+ °C | Usually |
		  | PC | 260–300+ | 100–120 | ~110–140 °C | Yes |
		- **PLA**
			- ✅ Easiest to print, stiff, sharp detail
			- ❌ Brittle, deforms in a hot car or sunlight
		- **PETG**
			- ✅ Tougher than PLA, chemical & water resistant
			- ❌ Stringing, can bond too strongly to PEI
		- **ABS**
			- ✅ Heat resistant, sandable, acetone smoothing
			- ❌ Warping, layer cracks, fumes
		- **ASA**
			- ✅ Like ABS + UV resistant, good outdoors
			- ❌ Warping, fumes
		- **TPU**
			- ✅ Flexible, rubbery
			- ❌ Print slowly, direct drive preferred
		- **Nylon (PA)**
			- ✅ Very tough, wear resistant
			- ❌ Absorbs moisture, must be dried, warps
		- **PC**
			- ✅ Strongest, most heat resistant
			- ❌ High temps, warping
	- ### Notes
		- **CF / GF blends** (e.g. PLA-CF, PA-CF) are stiffer but abrasive, so use a hardened steel nozzle
		- **Silk / Matte PLA** are mostly cosmetic and usually weaker than plain PLA
		- **Moisture** causes stringing, popping and weak parts. Dry Nylon, TPU and PETG before printing
		- Quick pick: PLA for prototypes, PETG for functional parts, ASA for outdoors/heat, TPU for flexible parts
