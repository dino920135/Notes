- ## Filaments
	- ### Comparison
		- | Filament | Nozzle / Bed (°C) | Softens at | Strengths | Downsides | Enclosure? |
		  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
		  | **PLA** | 190–220 / 50–60 | ~55–60 °C | Easiest to print, stiff, sharp detail | Brittle, deforms in a hot car or sunlight | No |
		  | **PETG** | 220–250 / 70–85 | ~75–80 °C | Tougher than PLA, chemical & water resistant | Stringing, can bond too strongly to PEI | No |
		  | **ABS** | 230–260 / 90–110 | ~100 °C | Heat resistant, sandable, acetone smoothing | Warping, layer cracks, fumes | Yes |
		  | **ASA** | 240–260 / 90–110 | ~100 °C | Like ABS + UV resistant, good outdoors | Warping, fumes | Yes |
		  | **TPU** | 210–230 / 30–60 | n/a | Flexible, rubbery | Print slowly, direct drive preferred | No |
		  | **Nylon (PA)** | 250–280 / 70–100 | ~80+ °C | Very tough, wear resistant | Absorbs moisture, must be dried, warps | Usually |
		  | **PC** | 260–300+ / 100–120 | ~110–140 °C | Strongest, most heat resistant | High temps, warping | Yes |
	- ### Notes
		- **CF / GF blends** (e.g. PLA-CF, PA-CF) are stiffer but abrasive, so use a hardened steel nozzle
		- **Silk / Matte PLA** are mostly cosmetic and usually weaker than plain PLA
		- **Moisture** causes stringing, popping and weak parts. Dry Nylon, TPU and PETG before printing
		- Quick pick: PLA for prototypes, PETG for functional parts, ASA for outdoors/heat, TPU for flexible parts
