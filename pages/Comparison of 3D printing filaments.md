- ## Filaments
	- ### Characteristics
		- How the printed part behaves
		- | Filament | Softens at | Stiffness | Toughness | Outdoor / UV |
		  |---|---|---|---|---|
		  | PLA | ~55–60 °C | High | Low | Poor |
		  | PETG | ~75–80 °C | Medium | Medium | Fair |
		  | ABS | ~100 °C | Medium | Medium | Poor |
		  | ASA | ~100 °C | Medium | Medium | Good |
		  | TPU | n/a | Flexible | Very high | Fair |
		  | Nylon | ~80+ °C | Medium | Very high | Fair |
		  | PC | ~110–140 °C | High | Very high | Fair |
			- **PLA**
			  collapsed:: true
				- ✅ Easiest to print, stiff, sharp detail
				- ❌ Brittle, deforms in a hot car or sunlight
			- **PETG**
			  collapsed:: true
				- ✅ Tougher than PLA, chemical & water resistant
				- ❌ Stringing, can bond too strongly to PEI
			- **ABS**
			  collapsed:: true
				- ✅ Heat resistant, sandable, acetone smoothing
				- ❌ Warping, layer cracks, fumes
			- **ASA**
			  collapsed:: true
				- ✅ Like ABS + UV resistant, good outdoors
				- ❌ Warping, fumes
			- **TPU**
			  collapsed:: true
				- ✅ Flexible, rubbery
				- ❌ Print slowly, direct drive preferred
			- **Nylon (PA)**
			  collapsed:: true
				- ✅ Very tough, wear resistant
				- ❌ Absorbs moisture, must be dried, warps
			- **PC**
			  collapsed:: true
				- ✅ Strongest, most heat resistant
				- ❌ High temps, warping
	- ### Moisture & Air Quality
		- | Filament | Absorbs moisture | Fumes / emissions | Main concern |
		  |---|---|---|---|
		  | PLA | Low | Low | Mild smell |
		  | PETG | Medium | Low | — |
		  | ABS | Low–Medium | High | Styrene |
		  | ASA | Low–Medium | High | Styrene, acrylonitrile |
		  | TPU | High | Medium | — |
		  | Nylon | Very high | Medium–High | Caprolactam |
		  | PC | Medium–High | Medium | BPA (some brands) |
		- **Moisture (hygroscopic)**
			- Wet filament → stringing, popping/hissing, bubbles, rough surface, weak layers
			- Store in a sealed box with desiccant, dry before printing (see [[Filament Printer Settings]])
			- High absorbers (Nylon, TPU) can get wet within hours in humid air
			- **Suitable storage humidity (RH)**
				- | Filament | Ideal RH | Max RH | Open air exposure |
				  |---|---|---|---|
				  | PLA | < 30% | 50% | Days–weeks OK |
				  | ABS / ASA | < 30% | 40–50% | Days OK |
				  | PETG | < 25% | 40% | 1–2 days |
				  | PC | < 20% | 30% | Hours–1 day |
				  | TPU | < 20% | 30% | Hours |
				  | Nylon | < 15% | 20% | Hours, print from a dry box |
				- Put a **hygrometer** in the storage box, replace / recharge desiccant when RH rises above the ideal
				- In humid climates indoor RH can be 60–80%, so don't leave spools out on the printer between prints
		- **Toxic air**
			- All filaments release **ultrafine particles (UFPs)** while printing, even PLA
			- ABS / ASA emit the most **VOCs** (volatile organic compounds), mainly styrene
			- CF / GF blends add fine fiber dust when sanding or cutting
			- Keep the printer out of bedrooms, ventilate the room, and wait a few minutes after a print before opening the enclosure
			- For ABS / ASA / Nylon / PC: use the enclosure + a **HEPA (particles) + activated carbon (VOCs)** filter, or vent outside
	- ### Notes
		- **CF / GF blends** (e.g. PLA-CF, PA-CF) are stiffer but abrasive, so use a hardened steel nozzle
		- **Silk / Matte PLA** are mostly cosmetic and usually weaker than plain PLA
		- Quick pick: PLA for prototypes, PETG for functional parts, ASA for outdoors/heat, TPU for flexible parts
